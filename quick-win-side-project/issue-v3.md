# Find a quick-win sale within 14 days

Find one recurring problem in the industry below that a side-project developer can solve, and sell within the Time to first sale in Inputs. The customer's gain from solving it must be measurable in money: win money, save money or time that can be translated directly in money. Do not taking into account regulation fine money.

The solution can be a document, a dataset, DFY(Done For You) or a tool.

Saying that no idea qualifies is a valid result. Never invent a problem to fill the report. Never reject an idea only because a fact could not be found: a missing fact is **unverified**, not **failed** (see Hard criteria).

The revenue can be around 500 euros per month. This is not a strict amount as the revenue can be irregular. It is given as indication only.

## Inputs

- **Run date:** today
- **Industry:** Routine cleaning of buildings (in French: NAF 81.21Z Nettoyage courant des bâtiments)
- **NAF codes:** 81.21Z 
- **Sale location:** Doubs (25), France
- **Time to first sale:** 14 days from the start of the work
- **Developer time:** evenings and weekends, at most 20 hours in those 14 days
- **Gain visible within:** 30 days after delivery, unless the gain can be calculated at delivery from the customer's own figures

- **Languages:** report in English; searches and customer-facing text in French

The NAF code is given as main industry(direction) but the customers can come from any industry that interract with the NAF code industry to solve a problem with the given industry above. For example the custoners can be the customers of the industry above, style B2B2C

## Definitions

- **Sale:** a written commitment (signed quote or order) with a price and a payment date. Payment can come later.
- **Payer:** the person who signs. By default, this is the owner or manager of a company classified under the NAF codes in Inputs, with about 1 to 50 employees. This includes:
  - independent companies;
  - franchisees or affiliates that operate under one of the networks in Inputs but are separate companies;
  - owners of several sites.

- **Network:** a national brand or group a prospect belongs to (see Networks in Inputs). The network often supplies software, booking, reminders and marketing. Check what the network already provides before assuming a gap.
- **Host:** a third party that gives access to customers, such as a federation, accountant, supplier, training body or software vendor (see Possible hosts in Inputs).
- **Free proof:** the real value given to a prospect at first contact, before anything is asked. See Step 3.
- **Starving crowd (Gary Halbert):** a segment with urgent demand that is poorly served and already spends money trying to solve the problem. It does not mean a segment without money.

## Ways to get money

The following are examples of ways to obtain money, but they are by no means exhaustive. You are free to explore other possibilities beyond those listed.
- **(a) Customer Acquisition:** new paying clients from any channel or stage of awareness.
- **(b) Average Order Value:** larger transactions or contracts at the point of sale.
- **(c) Purchase Frequency:** shorter re-order cycles or ongoing contractual loyalty.
- **(d) Direct Leakage Recovery:** capturing revenue already earned or lost to friction (uncollected invoices, unbilled services, churn).
- **(e) Net Margin Optimization:** pricing power, operational automation, lower cost-to-serve.
- **(f) Idle Capacity & Yield Optimization:** monetizing perishable, already-paid-for assets without raising fixed costs.
- **(g) Embedded Partnership & B2B2C Distribution:** near-zero CAC by using the trust, audience, and checkout flows of non-competing, complementary businesses.
- **(h) Financial Engineering & Cash Flow Acceleration:** shorter cash conversion cycle, better payment terms, embedded financing.
- **(i) Intellectual Property & Knowledge Monetization:** productizing tacit knowledge, workflows, or data into low-touch digital assets.
- **(j) Structural Business Model Arbitrage & Risk Reversal:** outcome-based pricing, or automated capture of non-dilutive capital (grants, tax credits, subsidies).


## The developer role

The developer is the information expert, not the domain expert. The developer's skills are:
- information management and architecture;
- content and data curation;
- AI, low-tech tools, coding AI agents and no-code.

The developer makes up for a domain expert's missing skills, or builds what the expert has no time to build.

Domain knowledge must come from sourced material, the customer, or a named expert. Never assume the developer has it. Domain data can enter the solution through imports or through forms with predefined values. The solution processes domain data to produce its output, but a domain expert validates that output. The solution may therefore produce a draft that the expert validates, to make the expert's work faster, cheaper and easier.

The developer never performs or signs any of the regulated acts in Inputs.

## Hard criteria

A candidate must meet all of these. Rate each criterion:

- **Met:** evidence supports it.
- **Unverified:** no evidence either way was found within budget. Turn it into a pilot test that the first outreach can answer.
- **Failed:** evidence contradicts it. Reject the candidate and name the criterion.

A candidate can have **at most one Unverified** criterion, and never on criterion 1 (Money) or criterion 7 (Developer fit).

1. **Money:** the gain is expressed in euros through at least one of these:
   - the customer already pays staff to do the work;
   - the customer outsources the work;
   - comparable customers elsewhere pay for a solution.

   Give the calculation. Each input is labelled **verified** (sourced), **inferred** (derived from a source) or **assumption**. At least one input must be verified. Give assumption inputs as a low–high range, and check the minimum gain at the **low** end of the range. If the outcome cannot be expressed in money at all, the criterion fails.
2. **Recurrence:** the problem occurs at least once a year and does not disappear after the first fix.
3. **Reason to act now:** the problem is live today or recurs within 90 days of the run date, so the payer has a reason to sign within the Time to first sale.
4. **Access:** The customer should be easy to contact.
5. **Build:** a first sellable version fits within the developer time in Inputs. It can be delivered partly by hand, and it can be reused or updated if the market grows. Here the idea is not to build a full software or ERP for the industry but look what can complement the existing solutions and is in real demand. It can be a document, dataset, content that improve the industry existing work. But avoid regulation fined.
6. **Developer fit:** the solution needs no domain expertise from the developer to be developped. If solution need expert data input or the ouput need to be validated the data and the validation are done by the expert. In this case the solution produce a draft, not a finished output.
8. **Market:** the market can be small and revenue can be irregular, because this is a market test. State how the market could grow, starting from the growth paths in Inputs. 

## Evidence rules

- Cite a source for each factual claim. Label each claim **verified**, **inferred**, or **assumption**.
- Treat vendor marketing, self-reported results, and anonymous reviews as weak evidence.
- "No evidence found" is never proof for or against a claim. Record it as such.
- If no search tool is available, say so, mark Discovery, Pre-check, and the adversarial test as not run, and do not call any idea validated.

## Step 1: Discovery

The solution to build should complement the existing established ecosystem/infrastructure already in place. It can be a document, a dataset or a tool that creates value to the existing tool not competing with them. 
Search in this order the following discovery options:

1. **Local and sector signals:** for companies in the industry, look for:
   - job adverts, using the job titles in Inputs;
   - trade press and regulator announcements, using the trade sources and regulators in Inputs;
   - forums and owner groups;
   - complaints;
   - vendor pricing pages for software and services sold to the prospect unit in Inputs.
2. **Starving crowd:** a segment within the industry with urgent, poorly served demand that already spends money. Start with the segments to test in Inputs, but do not assume they qualify.
3. **Elsewhere in the world :** similar problems in the comparable markets in Inputs. Record how each is solved and what people pay. Adapt any solution to French rules and to the sale location.

4. **Input/output quality:** Examine what data or content the industry needs as input to perform its tasks faster, more cost-effectively, efficiently, and effortlessly.

- Identify the outputs (data, content, products, or services) that the industry provides to downstream or adjacent industries, and determine how improving their quality, accessibility, or delivery can help those industries succeed.

5. **Reports and online content:** Research industry reports, government and local authority publications, news articles, academic research papers, case studies, Google Scholar, Google Alerts, and relevant books to identify unmet needs, inefficiencies, challenges, and recurring problems that represent potential business opportunities. Additionally, analyse online content, including customer reviews, complaints, discussions, and recurring negative feedback, to uncover real-world problems, pain points, and unmet demands that could be addressed through innovative products, services, or solutions.

State in the report which discovery option succeed.

For each candidate, look for timing leverage as of the run date. This can be a dated rule, deadline, budget cycle, seasonal peak, technology change, or local factor. Give a dated source, explain why it improves positioning now, and say when the window closes. If there is no leverage, say so.

## Step 2: Pre-check

For each candidate, verify and record with a source:

- the timing fact and its date;
- the French rules that allow or block the offer;
- what the main networks already provide for this problem;
- the number of reachable prospects in the sale location, with how they were counted;
- at least one example of someone paying for this outcome.

Rate every hard criterion as Met, Unverified or Failed. Reject any candidate with a Failed criterion or more Unverified criteria than allowed. These results are the **Pre-check facts**.

## Step 3: Real free proof and offer

For each remaining candidate, design the following.

**The free proof**
- It is delivered at first contact, with no call, sign-up, data, or purchase requested.
- It is real value of a kind customers would pay for. Cite an example and its price if one is found; otherwise label it **assumption** and explain why it has value.
- It is built only from public data, and it respects French rules on business prospecting (B2B email allowed if relevant to the recipient's role, with an opt-out) and on personal data (GDPR).
- It is cheap enough to produce for enough prospects within the developer time.

**The paid offer**
- It is the natural next step after the free proof, and it delivers something the free proof does not.
- It has a price and a payment date.
- It includes risk reversal in the sense used by Jay Abraham: the developer carries the risk, for example through payment after results or a guarantee. Guarantee conditions must depend only on what the developer controls.

## Step 4: Adversarial test: try to kill the idea

Run this after the Pre-check and the free proof design. Stop arguing for the idea and try to prove it wrong with a real search tool. Memory does not count. Do not search for the Pre-check facts again; the final recheck below is the only exception.

**Before searching**, write down for each attack the specific, observable finding that would kill the idea. It must be something a search could actually find (a product, a price, a rule, a documented statement), not an absence of data. Do not change it afterwards.

**Order:** run attacks 1 and 2 first as one batch. If any is fatal, stop and move to the next candidate. Run the rest only if none is fatal. Word each search to find disconfirming evidence, and write it in French, using French trade terms.

1. **Existing solutions:** does any of these already solve the problem well enough that prospects in the sale location would not pay?
   - a free tool;
   - a low-cost product;
   - a feature of software the prospects already use, such as the network's own tools, booking platforms, or accounting, invoicing or CRM software;
   - the host itself.

   Kill finding example: "a tool included in the network package or the usual sector software that does this, with no recent complaints found." Recent complaints about the existing solution, or a solution that only covers some networks, make this **weakened**, not fatal.

2. **Willingness to pay:** is there evidence that these customers refuse to pay, have no budget, do it in-house, or buy only through the network head office or a long procurement?
3. **Host reality (only if a host is proposed; otherwise N/A):** does the host sell a competing offer, or is it barred from commissions or from endorsing third parties? Is there evidence of such referrals?
4. **Recurring use:** is there evidence of low repeat use, high churn, or a problem that disappears after the first fix?
5. **Coming changes and timing:** is there an announced law, regulation (see Regulators and rules to watch in Inputs), network-wide rollout, or trend that shrinks the opportunity within 12 months? Are competitors already capturing the window? Cover only changes not in the Pre-check facts.
6. **Do-it-yourself with AI:** could the owner or an employee solve this in an afternoon with general AI tools? If yes, why would they keep paying?



**Final recheck.** For the surviving candidate, recheck the claims the recommendation depends on most (market size, pricing benchmarks, competitor claims). Confirm that each source says what you claim, and look for an independent second source.

**Rate each attack** as survives, weakened, or fatal. Finding nothing is "no evidence found", not support.

- **Fatal:** the finding written in advance was found. Reject the idea, record the killing finding, and test the next-best candidate within budget.
- **Weakened:** partial evidence was found. Redesign the offer, or state the risk and add a pilot test for it.

Surviving the attacks does not prove demand. Only a sale does.

If every candidate is rejected or the budget runs out, report that no idea qualifies.
